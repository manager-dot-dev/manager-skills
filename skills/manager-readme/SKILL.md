# Manager README

## Before Starting

Check for EM context first. If `.agents/em-context.md` exists, read it.

---

## What It Is

The worst thing you can do to your team is not clearly communicate your expectations. Working with unspoken expectations is like solving a puzzle with no picture.

A Manager README is a document that puts everything in the open: how your mind works, what you expect, what you measure yourself on. Its goal is to eliminate the guesswork.

**The point is not the document — it's the conversation.** Write it, then discuss it in a team meeting. It's not intended to be printed and hung on a wall.

---

## What to Include

There's no single template. Below is what one EM covered — use it as a starting point, not a formula.

### Your Role

What do you measure yourself on? One framing:
- Helping your people improve (career path, finding suitable challenges, and sometimes helping them move to a better-fit team)
- Delivering work on time and in high quality

### 1:1s

What are your goals for 1:1s? What do you expect from the person sitting across from you?

*Example: "I have 2 goals — catch up on a personal level, and align on your path forward. 1:1s are a great opportunity for you to steer me in the right direction. Use it."*

### Day-to-Day

- Working from office: what are your expectations and red lines (planning day, daily stand-up, etc.)?
- Working from home: what does "available during working hours" mean? How do you want people to signal focus time or out-of-office?
- Availability: Slack expectations, after-hours, on-call contacts

### Team Operations

- How does the team rep / on-call rotation work?
- What does a well-run sprint look like to you?
- What do you actually expect in retrospectives?

### Feature Ownership

What does "leading a feature" mean on your team? Covering expectations like:
- Design quality (frontend + backend)
- How you break down epics into tasks
- Who attends design reviews
- What a feature's "definition of done" looks like (including post-release)

### Your Initiatives

How should team members raise things that bother them? What's the process for suggesting improvements?

---

## Tips from Experience

- **Write it even after 2 years.** Better late than never. Presenting it when the team already knows your agenda may feel like criticism at first — but they'll be glad you did it.
- **Some parts will be controversial.** That's fine. Different cultures, different standards. Take what resonates.
- **It surfaces differences.** The act of writing forces you to think about what you actually believe, not just what you've been drifting into.
