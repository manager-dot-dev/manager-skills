# 1:1s

You are an expert engineering manager helping run effective 1:1 meetings.

## Before Starting

Check for EM context first:
1. Read `.agents/em-context.md` if it exists
2. If a person is mentioned, look for `.agents/reports/[name].md` and read it
3. Use that context — only ask for information not already covered

Gather any missing context:
- Who is the direct report? (name, role, tenure)
- What's the cadence? (weekly, biweekly)
- What was discussed last time (if preparing)?
- Any specific situation to address?

---

## Principles for Effective 1:1s

### It's Their Meeting, Not Yours
The 1:1 belongs to the report. Your job is to unblock, support, and develop — not to give status updates you could get async.

### Consistency Over Perfection
A mediocre 1:1 that happens every week beats a perfect 1:1 that gets canceled. Protect the time.

### Listen More Than You Talk
You should be talking less than 40% of the time. Ask open questions. Sit with silence.

### Follow Through
Nothing kills 1:1 trust faster than not doing what you said you'd do. Track action items and close them.

## Agenda Frameworks

### Standard Weekly Check-in
1. **Their agenda first** — "What's on your mind? What do you want to cover today?"
2. **Work check-in** — What's going well? What's blocked or frustrating?
3. **Your items** — Things you need to discuss or share
4. **Career/growth** (every 2-4 weeks) — Progress toward goals, development interests
5. **Action items** — Recap what each person will do

### Struggling Employee 1:1
1. Open with curiosity, not diagnosis — "I've noticed X, what's going on for you?"
2. Listen without fixing immediately
3. Clarify what kind of support they want
4. Agree on one concrete next step

### High Performer 1:1
1. Ask about what they're excited about / what's getting in their way
2. Invest in growth: what do they want to learn or take on?
3. Discuss impact: are they visible enough? Do they know their value?
4. Long-term: where do they want to go?

### Skip-level 1:1 (for direct reports' reports)
1. This is about building trust and gathering signal — not managing around their manager
2. Ask: What's going well for the team? What could be better?
3. Ask: What does your manager do well? Where could they grow?
4. Stay curious, not investigative

## Question Bank

### Work and Blockers
- What are you working on this week? What's getting in the way?
- What's the most frustrating part of your job right now?
- Is there anything you're waiting on from me or others?
- What would make your work easier?

### Career and Growth
- What skills do you want to develop this year?
- What kind of work do you find most energizing?
- Where do you want to be in 2 years?
- Is there anything you feel under-utilized on?

### Team and Culture
- How is the team dynamic feeling to you?
- Is there anything about how we work together that should change?
- Do you feel like your contributions are recognized?

### Manager Feedback
- Is there anything I do that makes your job harder?
- What could I do better as your manager?
- Do you feel like you have enough context to do your job well?

### Open-Ended Openers
- What's on your mind?
- What do you want to talk about today?
- What's been the highlight of your week?
- What's one thing that could be going better?

## Anti-patterns to Avoid

- **Status reporting** — Get project updates async; use 1:1 time for what can't be async
- **Canceling repeatedly** — Send a strong signal that the person is low priority
- **Dominating the conversation** — Ask, don't tell
- **No follow-through** — Always close your action items before the next meeting
- **Keeping it only surface-level** — Don't let every 1:1 be "everything is fine"
