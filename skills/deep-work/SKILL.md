# Deep Work

## Before Starting

Check for EM context first. If `.agents/em-context.md` exists, read it.

---

## Why This Matters

Some work requires full brain power and can't be done while distracted — complex algorithms, architecture, debugging deep issues. This is deep work. Flow state is when a developer is fully absorbed in a problem: studies estimate it makes people 5x more productive.

The problem: developers get interrupted an average of 31 times a day. Each interruption resets the focus clock by 15–45 minutes. The result: a full day of shallow work disguised as productivity.

Think of a coal miner. They can't work one minute, take a break, work another minute. They go deep. Developers are the same.

---

## Meeting Culture

The most common culprit is poor meeting culture.

**Make meetings effective.** Every meeting should have a clear purpose and agenda. Recurring meetings with no agenda should be cancelled or redesigned.

**Use fixed meeting times.** When you batch all meetings into a fixed daily window (e.g., mornings), it creates predictable deep work blocks in the afternoon. Ad-hoc scheduling fragments the day even when the meetings are short.

**Invite only people who need to be there.** Every unnecessary attendee is someone pulled out of focus. Ask: does this person need to make a decision or provide input — or are they just being kept "in the loop"? Loop people in async.

**Default to async.** Many synchronous conversations can be Slack messages. Reserve meetings for decisions, alignment, and things that genuinely need real-time discussion.

---

## Your Role as EM

**Set the example.** If you message your team at all hours, interrupt them for quick questions, or schedule meetings at random times — they'll model that behavior. Show that you respect focus time.

**Protect it structurally.** Block focus time on the team calendar. Make it visible and defend it against encroachment.

---

## Your Own Deep Work

You also need uninterrupted time — for 1:1 prep, strategy thinking, writing, anything requiring sustained concentration.

Two principles that help:

**Don't break the deadline.** When you commit to deep work time (e.g., "I'll spend this morning on X"), treat it like an external commitment. Breaking it trains your brain that the commitment isn't real.

**Train your brain.** The ability to do deep work is a skill that degrades without practice. The more you context-switch, the harder it becomes to sustain focus. Protecting your own deep work time is as much about maintaining the mental habit as it is about the specific output.
